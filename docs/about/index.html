About · 18 October 2026

An independent project, at an early stage

UDeep is the work of Mathew Newman (ORCID 0000-0003-1784-3250). It is an independent project, not affiliated with an institution or a standards body. The profile is a working draft, and the tools are a research preview. Where it is going is on the roadmap.

The missing evidence

A study that has been designed and not yet run

No one has shown that HDR helps people read a scientific figure. The planned study compares three versions of the same data, with tasks that have known answers:

  • SDR clipped: the ordinary figure, with everything above the knee white.
  • SDR compressed: the whole range squeezed into SDR. This is the best SDR alternative.
  • HDR: the ordinary figure plus the range above white.

The predictions, to be registered in advance: above the knee, HDR does at least as well as the compressed figure, and both beat the clipped one. Below the knee, HDR equals the clipped figure, and both beat the compressed one. Reading values from the colour bar is no worse with HDR. If these hold, HDR is the only version that does not trade one range against the other. A null result would be published too.

It needs research-ethics approval before anyone is recruited, and it would benefit from a collaborator in vision science or in a field that reads such images every day. If that is you, please get in touch.

Citing and reuse

How to cite it

Until a versioned release with a DOI exists, cite the repository and the profile version:

Newman, M. (2026). UDeep: Ultra Deep Range scientific figures and the UDeep Figure Profile, draft v0.3.
https://github.com/ultradeeprange/udeep

The repository's CITATION.cff carries the same information in machine-readable form. Code is under BSD-3-Clause; text, the profile and this site are under CC BY 4.0. Figures made from third-party data keep the terms of their sources, listed on the gallery page.
